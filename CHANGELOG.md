# Changelog

Kokuhaku runs on Minecraft 1.20.1 (Fabric), launched through Prism Launcher.
Versions before 1.3.0 weren't tagged at the time; their entries were written up afterwards.

## Unreleased

### Fixed
- The hospital's upper floors couldn't be reached. Both flights of stairs were built in the same place, so the second filled the headroom of the first, and you couldn't climb past the fourth step. The flights now sit either side of the walkway, so the records room on the top floor, and the diary page in it, can be found again.
- Hours in a hospital bed, or the two hours she takes from you, skipped straight over the end of school. Detention you weren't there for followed you into the next day, and on a note day the note in your coat pocket was lost. Detention is let off now, and the note is in your pocket when you're back on your feet.
- "Please stop. I won't ask again." was forgotten on Continue, so going back to her was safe after all. It's kept with the run now.
- On "We're staying in today" weekends with her, she led you out on her errands at nine and then broke your arm for being out. She stays in too now.
- The blackout could send her after the one she'd promised to leave alone, or after someone lying in a hospital bed, and spend her one chance of the night on them.
- If the girl who asked you a favour died, the favour stayed on the screen for the rest of the run and nobody else would ask you one. It's dropped now.
- In a party, two of you could ask a teacher to make up for a mark at once and take two off, or more than you had.
- In a party, lunch from the counter could put the wallet in debt if someone else spent the money while you were choosing. You get water instead now.
- A friend in your party who didn't come back on Continue lost everything in their bag at the next morning's save. Their bag is kept now, and anyone who leaves mid-run keeps theirs too.
- A friend joining after Continue could be woken while a night was going by, and the night could run twice. They wait now, and joining in the afternoon doesn't give them a second morning.
- A friend who said yes to a sleepover and then left was still "at hers" when they came back, so their own bed wasn't theirs and getting home before curfew didn't count.
- When the last one still answering in a lesson left, the others had to sit out the rest of it.
- The bag's days off and days late could change for days already gone when someone died, and a friend's death made people grieve for them on days before it happened.
- Some stares and secrets were never written in your bag: two of them could share the same note.
- Students waiting a long time at a red light gave up as if stuck, and hopped sideways or across the road when it changed.
- Doors students opened on the way somewhere stayed open after they got there.
- After the bedroom light went off, nothing in the room had moved if something already had that day.
- A photo taken at the moment a scene started could leave the clock, money and phone hidden for the rest of the run.
- The mute key did nothing during scenes, walks, grabs and the end.
- Disconnecting while talking could leave your microphone open.
- The bedroom light could come on twice more after every Continue, and the clearing in the forest could be found again. Both are kept with the run now: twice a run means twice a run.
- The footsteps behind you in the forest never stopped as long as you kept walking. They give up after a while now, and then it's quiet for a bit.
- On the title screen, a friend's menu stayed open after they'd left, and could act on someone else's box. It closes when the party changes.

## 1.5.1 — 23 November 2026

### Fixed
- Some classmates never turned up to school. Twenty-one homes put the family somewhere with no headroom, so they were pushed up onto the roof and stayed there all day. They start indoors now.
- Anyone who got stuck on the way somewhere, on a table at home or behind something in the street, kept trying the same way for ever. Now, if nobody can see them, they get there in the end, as people do; if you're watching, they step back and try another way.
- A crossing could stay on red for good if someone stood close to the stripes, and everyone waiting there waited all day. Only people actually on the crossing hold the lights now, and never for long.

## 1.5.0 — 22 November 2026

### New
- **Food and water.** Two bars under your money run down through the day, water faster than food. Eat breakfast at home, lunch at school, buy drinks from the machines, or eat in town. Run low and you slow down; run out and you can barely stand.
- **Places to eat in town.** Ramen Kazu on the shopping street, a soba stand by the station and a café on the beach. Each has its own menu, prices and opening hours, and sitting down to eat takes a while.
- **Pocket money.** ¥2,000 on the kitchen table every Monday. It has to last the week.
- **Clothes.** Kimura Clothing on the shopping street sells five outfits. You change into yours after school and at weekends. Something smart helps on a date, and the long black coat makes you hard to follow after dark.
- **Snow days.** Some runs it snows in the night and the school shuts for the day, or two.
- **Getting a girlfriend is much harder, as it is in real life.** She has to know you, so talk to her on different days, not just a lot on one. Hang out as friends first. Ask too soon and she says no, the whole class hears about it, and you can't ask again for days; a second no is final. Some girls only ever want to be friends, and nothing tells you which. Once you're together, keep talking to her, or she'll end it.
- **More on the phone.** Missed calls in the night, and some that ring while you're awake. Voice messages you can play. Your location, sent back to you. Her contact renames itself. She shows as online when she's close, and "last seen" when she isn't. And now and then she lets something slip about herself.
- Screenshots on the GitHub page.
- **The diary knows the town.** Press In town at the top of your diary to see when Ramen Kazu, Station Soba, Umi no Ie and Kimura Clothing open and shut, when they stop taking orders, where the shifts are and when the pocket money comes. Lessons takes you back. Days already gone this week are greyed out, and a day the school was shut for snow says Closed. A snow day still to come isn't in it.
- **The register.** A few minutes into homeroom, if you're in 2A, the teacher takes the register. Most mornings it goes by without a word. After someone from the class is gone, the teacher gets to their name once, stops, and goes on to the next; after that there are just names that get skipped. Some mornings someone says "Here" for you. And one morning in the second week there's a name at the end that nobody in the class has. It never says who's missing among the living. Not on festival day.
- **Your bedroom light is on when you come home.** Once in a while, from day 4, out late with nobody about, the light in your room comes on while you're away. Get to your front door with no girl anywhere near you and you hear the switch, and it goes off just before you walk in. Get in before that and you just don't remember leaving it on. Twice a run at most, between all of you.
- **You can hear curfew coming.** In the last quarter of an hour before curfew, out on your own, you hear steps somewhere behind you. A few more each minute, and closer. They stop when you stop. In a party each of you hears only your own.
- **You can pick up.** When the unknown number rings of an evening, get your phone out before it stops and you've answered. Nobody speaks. You hear someone breathing, somewhere with a fire going, for a few seconds, and then they hang up. The call stays in her chat. In a party only the one who picks up hears it; everyone else's stops ringing. Ghosts, anyone watching and anyone in the middle of something can't answer.
- **People talk about each other.** Stand near someone with nothing to tell you and now and then you'll overhear them on about one of the girls: where she lives, her club, who she's been seen with, something odd she did. It's all true of someone, and it could be about any of them. What you overhear goes in your bag. Twice a day at most.
- **Ask about her.** Pick a girl under Suspects and press Ask about her. The next person you talk to, boy or girl, you can ask what they make of her. Someone in her group will tell you where she lives and what club she's in. Everyone else tells you what they've heard, or what they think, which isn't always much. Ask the same person twice in a day and they've already told you.
- **Your own suspect board.** Under Suspects, mark each girl Her?, Not sure or Not her, and write yourself a line about her. The board is saved with the run and kept on Continue. In a party everyone keeps their own, and nobody else sees it. It replaces Cross off.
- **Every note in your bag is dated.** Things you write down now say which day you got them. Notes from a run saved before this stay undated.
- **Her clues.** Pick a girl under Suspects and press Her clues to see only what names her, or what she told you. Show all puts the rest back.
- **Three girls with secrets of their own.** Each run, three girls in your class have somewhere they'd rather nobody saw them go. One goes to the hospital in the evenings. One walks on her own late at night. One waits at the end of your road after school and looks away when you see her. Catch them at it and you make a note. Any of them could be her, and you only learn what they were doing when it's over. A run saved before this gets its three on Continue.
- **Someone else likes you too.** One of the girls who isn't her has taken a liking to you, and some nights she leaves something at your door: sweets, a drawing on the window, a tidied room. It looks just like what she does. Now and then a note under your door is written the way that girl would write it. You find out who left what when it's over.
- **Girls who like you text you.** Get on well enough with a girl and some evenings she'll text you, under her own name: goodnight, something about your day, now and then something that makes you check the door. Two of them a night at most. You can't answer these. Later in the month they get a little more worried about you.
- **Girlfriends get clingier.** Your girlfriend's goodnight text changes from night to night, and after the first week she wants to know where you've been and who with.
- **The class chat types.** Now and then, any time between seven in the morning and half eleven at night, or curfew if that's earlier, someone in the class chat starts typing, and the chat says who. Sometimes it comes to something. Sometimes they stop and nothing comes. Anyone in the class could be typing, and never while the unknown number is. Later in the month, now and then, it's someone who's dead. It says Unknown, and nothing ever comes.
- **The night types itself out.** What happened while you slept comes up on the black a letter at a time, with the click of keys, and stays up long enough to read. Three nights a run, from the fifth on, a line starts that never gets finished: it's typed half out, then deleted. In a party, what you heard at your own door is yours alone; nobody else sees it.
- **Lying awake for the others.** In a party, if you're in bed waiting for everyone else, the house makes its noises: the front door, something on the stairs, your phone buzzing with nothing on it, someone breathing. Only you hear them. Twice a night at most.
- **Phones come out when yours buzzes.** When a message comes in, someone standing near you might get their own phone out a moment later, look, and put it away. When it's the class chat, a few of them do. It could be anyone about, boys too, and never whoever's talking to you. In a party each of you sees your own.
- **Crossings chirp.** While the green man's on, the lights give a two-note chirp if you're near enough to hear it. After ten at night they go quiet. And once in a while, late on, on your own and watching from along the road, a crossing changes for nobody: amber, then red, chirping, with no one at the kerb and no one on the stripes. Once a night at most, for each of you.
- **Days off with the class.** On a weekend or a snow day, two of the friend groups say in the class chat in the morning where they're going: the station, the shops, the beach, the park or the field, at eleven. That's where they go, unless the weather sends them under a roof. Turn up where two of them are, between half ten and five, and they wave you over. Stay a while and it counts as a day you talked to each girl there; on your own, the time goes by. Once a day each. Which groups go out is down to the run, and she's in one of them no more often than anyone.
- **The eateries remember you.** Eat somewhere three times and they know you: the greeting changes, and what you had last time is at the top of the menu as The usual. In a party each of you is remembered on your own. And now and then, after a meal, whoever's serving mentions a girl from your class who was in that day. It could be any of them, and it's the same girl all day at that place. It doesn't go in your bag.
- **When it's over, you see when you could have known.** Once her name is out, the end tells you the day everything you needed had been said by someone, what your own board said about her and when you put it there, how many other girls you'd had down for it, and what was really behind one of the rumours you picked up. At most six of these, and anything else it has to tell you comes in among them. It says nothing while your friends are still out there.

### Changed
- **Jobs have a rota.** Each job gives you one shift a day; come back the same day and they tell you to come tomorrow. In a party, each of you has your own. Someone from school is on with you, and after the shift you're told who. Get through it without too many mistakes and they might tell you about a girl in your class, or something they saw from behind the counter, which goes in your bag. It could be about anyone.
- **Clothes each do something, and people notice.** The long black coat makes you hard to follow at night, but it isn't one for a date. A hoodie does the same, less so; in a party, whoever's least hidden decides. In a tracksuit, running home after curfew, you don't have to go quite as fast for it to count as running. The first time someone sees you in something new, they say so, each in their own way.
- **You're told to eat.** On the first walk in, he tells you to eat and drink more than you think you need, and where. When you run low, the corner of the screen says so, and on Easy and Medium the arrow points to the nearest vending machine. If you went to bed hungry or thirsty, breakfast doesn't put it right, and you're told in the morning. On a snow day the corner of the screen says the school's shut. How to Play says more about food and water.
- **A night costs what the night was.** Go to bed early and you wake up hungrier and thirstier than if you'd stayed out till curfew. Breakfast gives you more to drink than it did.
- **Snow days feel like snow.** Out in it, everyone puts their umbrella up and you can see their breath. Cars take the roads slower, and fewer people bother going out. Once of a snowy evening, out on your own, you might see a breath in the air somewhere behind you, where nobody's standing.
- **Some of the footsteps are real.** When a figure is standing still somewhere behind you, looking your way, the steps you hear as you walk come from where it is, and nothing else does. Stop, and there's one more. Turn round, and they stop. In a party, only the one it's watching hears them.
- **A quiet after every scare.** After a fright, a chase, or a figure that's suddenly not there any more, nothing else for a couple of minutes: no steps, no breathing, no doors.
- **Not everything on the floor is hers.** On Medium and up, another girl in your class loses things too: a hair clip in her club room, a pencil case by the shoe lockers. They turn up on the days between hers and look just the same. Whatever they say is true of the girl who dropped them. You find out whose they were when it's over. A run saved before this picks them up on any of those days still to come.
- **Anyone might know something, her included.** Rumours can be about any girl in the class, and there are more of them, and more notes in your locker. Some people who swear for a friend aren't quite sure of it. On Hard and Impossible, now and then, one of them is wrong. A run saved before this keeps the clues it was dealt.
- **What you glimpse in the dark fits more than one girl.** On Medium and up, the hair you catch at the window, the strands torn off in a struggle, the figure in your photo strip: whatever you're left with is true of her, and of at least two other girls still in your class whenever that's possible.
- **Off sick, or in late: the same for everyone.** Any girl can stay home for the day, her included, and some who were off first thing turn up around the first break and do the rest of the day as normal. Someone who comes in late won't walk you in that morning. If you're in the second lesson, you'll hear who came in late. Your bag counts days off and days late. Whoever you live with still walks in beside you.
- **You know how long it took.** After a meal, an afternoon out with a girl, or a date on your own, you're told how long went by and what time it is now. If home's not far off, it says by when, and once it's dark it says that too. If a lesson cuts it short, it tells you what really went by. Under ten minutes, or once curfew's come, it says nothing.
- **A date doesn't eat a party's afternoon.** In a party, going out with a girl no longer moves everyone's clock on by two hours. Like a meal, it's a moment in the dark for you, and the time is yours alone.
- **Going out with someone is a relationship.** Hang out with another girl while you're seeing someone, or be nice to one where your girlfriend can see, and she hears about it: she likes you a little less, and the next time you stop to talk she says something about it. The class notices too. A couple of days in, someone in the class chat saw the two of you walking home; a few days after that, if things aren't going well, they wonder if you're still together. It's the same for every girlfriend.
- A box of sweets means less from someone she barely knows.
- Compliments only win her over once she knows you.

### Fixed
- Pressing Escape could close a question the game was waiting on, and then nothing moved on: the night after the 3:12 nightmare never ended, her questions in a lesson stopped the clock, and in the hospital the camera froze. Questions you have to answer now stay until you do.
- Asking every girl out early gave her away, because only she could say yes. She now has to get to know you the same as anyone.
- Right-clicking a figure in the dark started an ordinary conversation, with her name on it. Figures and shapes also showed her real hair and glasses. They don't now.
- Dying on your own didn't end the run: Continue brought you back that morning. It ends it now. In a party, Continue no longer brings the taken back alive, or makes them ghosts for nothing.
- Continue wiped your phone, your progress in No One Can Save You, the radio, the things moved in your room and more. They're kept now.
- A save that couldn't be read left the menu stuck on black.
- A date late in the evening ran past curfew, and you were taken. Dates and hanging out now finish in good time.
- Meals, dates and shifts could skip straight over lessons, the blackout and the festival fireworks, which then never happened. Time now passes properly while you're busy.
- In No One Can Save You, a snow day early in the run crashed the game.
- A train ride could kill you as you got off at the festival platform.
- Classmates sat on each other, and on your chair, when their timetables sent them to your room.
- Teachers holding chalk flickered through every girl's face in multiplayer.
- A ghost called back after being taken was left stuck in her room's chair.
- The film at the end ran off the bottom of the screen.
- Skipping the walk to school left the camera walking the empty route.
- The launcher took any other Minecraft window for its own game and gave up. Now it only counts the game it started. If Prism is already running another game, it says so instead of doing nothing. Prism windows it hid could also stay hidden for good.
- The diary cut long subject names short.
- About three hundred smaller things besides: wrong text, numbers that didn't match, things that happened twice or not at all, and things in multiplayer that happened to the wrong person.

## 1.4.2 — 6 November 2026

### New
- **She types before she sends.** "typing…" comes and goes in bursts before her message arrives, in the chat and in your list of chats. Some nights she types for a while and nothing comes.
- **Messages she takes back.** Later in the month, some of what she sends turns into "This message was deleted" a minute after it arrives. Near the end, something you sent says "You deleted this message". You didn't.
- **Photos of you.** Now and then she sends a picture of you, taken from behind. If the screen flickers out of your own eyes for a moment, that's when she took it.
- **Names in the class chat.** Every message has a classmate's name on it, in their own colour. The ones who've died stop posting. Mostly.
- **Notifications.** A message slides in at the top of the screen when it arrives, so you know she's written without getting your phone out.
- **Ghosts can write to the living.** In the class chat a ghost can pick up to four words and post them, once every half a minute. The living see them from "Unknown", with most of the letters lost.

### Fixed
- As a ghost, some messages on the phone turned green or yellow partway through.

## 1.4.1 — 6 November 2026

### Faster
- **The game starts quicker.** The first time you open the launcher, it downloads Java 21 (about 50 MB, once) and runs Minecraft on that instead of Java 17. If the download fails, the game starts on Prism's own Java as before.
- Computers with plenty of memory give the game more of it, so worlds load faster.
- Added ModernFix, which cuts loading time and memory use.

### Fixed
- A change to the mods or settings now always reinstalls the game, even when Kokuhaku itself hasn't changed.

## 1.4.0 — 6 November 2026

### New
- **Save and Continue.** Your run saves every morning. Quit whenever you like, and **Continue** on the menu puts you back in bed that morning. There's one save slot: you can't start a new run until the saved one is finished.
- **Traffic lights** at every crossing. Cars stop at red, and for anyone on the stripes whatever the lights say. People wait at the kerb for their turn. Anywhere else in the road, the cars still don't stop for you.
- **Everyone wears the uniform** in multiplayer. Your friends look like classmates, not their Minecraft skins, told apart by their hair: dark, chestnut or fair.
- **A new phone.** Your chats in a list, each one a thread of bubbles: theirs grey, yours green with ticks for sent, delivered and read. New messages turn up while it's open, and you can keep walking while you read. When something's close behind you, the screen tears. In No One Will Save You it never finds a signal. The dead can still read their messages, but not every letter.
- Walking looks more natural: arms swing further forward than back and hang away from the body, feet turn out a little, and the stride has some weight in it. Nobody bounces along when they're frightened.

### Fixed
- Cars drove over crossings while people were still on them.
- Garbled characters on the morning banner and in a few other messages.

## 1.3.0 — 5 November 2026

### New
- **Watch a solo run.** If one person is playing and anyone else picks Spectator, the spectators see the whole run through that player's eyes. With two or more players, spectators are still ghosts.

### Fixed
- Sitting down and sneaking at the same moment could drop you through the floor.
- Getting up from a chair could put you through a wall, in a different room. You now stand up where you were standing when you sat down.
- Walking to school in multiplayer: both players got the same companions, who were dragged along two routes at once. That made them move too fast and walk through walls. Each companion now walks with one player only.
- The forest's scares now trigger reliably among tall trees.
